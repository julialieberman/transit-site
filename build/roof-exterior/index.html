Air conditioningThe rooftop air conditionerReported complete

The installation record lists an EcoCool 15K BTU, 110 V rooftop air conditioner with a heat pump and soft start.

The installer record groups the air conditioner with the roof rack and solar work. The unit, roof gasket, frame, soft start, and installation labor are recorded even though the exact free-blow or ducted variant still needs to be read from the nameplate.

The next useful documentation is practical rather than cosmetic: identify the exact model, trace its 110 V supply, observe condensate drainage, and record startup and running current.

Installed by Dan Moore in Salt Lake City.

WindowAdding the driver-side slider windowReported complete

A driver-side forward half-slider added daylight, ventilation, and a view from the living area.

The recorded installation used a VWD forward crew half-slider with Trim-Lok edge trim and Sikaflex automotive urethane. It is one of the most visible changes to the original cargo shell.

The public photo shows the installed window from inside the van. A future inspection still needs to document the product label, cut edge, urethane cure, drainage, opening travel, and leak performance.

Installed by Dan Moore in Salt Lake City.

SolarThree solar panels on the roofReported complete

The receipt records three 195 W monocrystalline split-cell panels mounted with the roof-rack work.

If the receipt's 195 W description matches the physical labels, the three panels would form a nominal 585 W array, close to the earlier rounded description of about 600 W. The operator also remembers 200 W as possible, so the exact rating remains open.

Before any expansion or rewiring, the physical labels, open-circuit voltage, series-or-parallel configuration, cable route, and charge-controller limits need to be recorded. The array must be treated as live whenever it is exposed to light.

Installed by Dan Moore in Salt Lake City.
