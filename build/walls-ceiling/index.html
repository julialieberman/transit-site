Headliner shelfMy first finished project: the headliner shelfReported complete

The VanEssential shelf became the first hands-on piece of the conversion I carried all the way through.

It started as a straightforward storage upgrade above the cab: assemble the shelf, fit the brackets, and use the existing Transit mounting area. The hardware assembly went together, but one body opening was not threaded the way the other side was.

A drill-and-tap tool broke in the opening, and the first extractor did not remove it. The mounting point eventually received a supplied rivnut and a 1/4-20 bolt. I finished the installation, but the repaired attachment stays unloaded until its seating, corrosion protection, and load path are inspected.

The van arrived with a completed floor assembly, which immediately made the cargo space more usable.

The floor is complete and appears to be in generally good condition. It is now the working surface for laying out templates, parts, and tools.

InsulationInsulation before the interior finishReported complete

A pre-cut 3M Thinsulate kit and sound-deadening material were installed throughout the cargo area.

The receipt record separates the insulation kit, sound-deadening material, and labor. That makes this one of the better documented early systems even though the exact product number and hidden coverage still need a physical check.

The visible cavities show insulation in place, but future finish work still needs to preserve access long enough to check gaps, compression, moisture condition, and every system that will disappear behind panels.

Installed by Dan Moore in Salt Lake City.

WindowAdding the driver-side slider windowReported complete

A driver-side forward half-slider added daylight, ventilation, and a view from the living area.

The recorded installation used a VWD forward crew half-slider with Trim-Lok edge trim and Sikaflex automotive urethane. It is one of the most visible changes to the original cargo shell.

The public photo shows the installed window from inside the van. A future inspection still needs to document the product label, cut edge, urethane cure, drainage, opening travel, and leak performance.

Installed by Dan Moore in Salt Lake City.

In progressPlanning the ceiling supportsIn progress

I have the AVC RIG ceiling-furring brackets and wall-to-ceiling supports. I am checking the parts and their installation order before attaching them permanently.

The parts are intended to turn the curved Transit shell into a flatter, serviceable surface for the finished ceiling and upper wall transition. The current work is identification and sequencing, not a finished installation.

The exact EXT kit contents, rear trim piece, fasteners, corrosion isolation, wiring clearance, and relationship to the separate cabinet structure remain open. Permanent attachment waits until those questions are resolved.

The separately recorded GoCode full interior trim kit is included here as related finish work. It is not part of the AVC ceiling-support kit or the GoCode Innie sleeper pair used in the bed discussion.

In progressWhy the cabinet braces are on holdIn progress

I started installing the Timber overhead cabinet structural kit, then paused to resolve fastening problems and its overlap with the ceiling supports.

The steel structure is meant to create a straight attachment line for future overhead cabinets. Holes were started for the supplied rivets, but the manual rivet tool did not work and no completed brace installation is recorded.

That pause is useful. Before work resumes, the exact kit revision, every drilled location, the rivet material and grip range, hidden-utility clearance, bare-edge treatment, and interaction with the AVC ceiling parts all need to be resolved.
