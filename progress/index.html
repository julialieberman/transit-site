Headliner shelf

Complete

The first thing I finally finished

The VanEssential shelf became the first hands-on piece of the conversion I carried all the way through.

It started as a straightforward storage upgrade above the cab: assemble the shelf, fit the brackets, and use the existing Transit mounting area. The hardware assembly went together, but one body opening was not threaded the way the other side was.

That turned a small installation into the first real problem-solving story of the build. A drill-and-tap tool broke, the first extractor did not solve it, and the mounting point ultimately received a supplied rivnut and a 1/4-20 bolt. The shelf is recorded as complete, while the repaired attachment stays unloaded until its seating, corrosion protection, and load path are inspected.

Heat

Complete

The under-seat heater established the cold-weather plan

An Espar AS3-B2L gasoline air heater is recorded under the passenger seat.

The installation record describes the heater, controller location, under-floor power routing, and warm-air outlet behind the seat base. It is one of the largest single climate-control investments in the build.

Because it is fuel-fired, the story is not finished at installation. The exact kit, fuel pickup, intake, exhaust, floor sealing, and commissioning remain inspection items, and the van needs working carbon-monoxide protection before the heater is relied on.

Solar

Complete

Three roof panels created the van's solar starting point

The receipt records three 195 W monocrystalline split-cell panels mounted with the roof-rack work.

If the receipt's 195 W description matches the physical labels, the three panels would form a nominal 585 W array, close to the earlier rounded description of about 600 W. The operator also remembers 200 W as possible, so the exact rating remains open.

Before any expansion or rewiring, the physical labels, open-circuit voltage, series-or-parallel configuration, cable route, and charge-controller limits need to be recorded. The array must be treated as live whenever it is exposed to light.

Cabinet bracing started, then stopped for a better plan

The Timber overhead cabinet structural kit is owned, and an installation attempt exposed unresolved fastening and overlap questions.

The steel structure is meant to create a straight attachment line for future overhead cabinets. Holes were started for the supplied rivets, but the manual rivet tool did not work and no completed brace installation is recorded.

That pause is useful. Before work resumes, the exact kit revision, every drilled location, the rivet material and grip range, hidden-utility clearance, bare-edge treatment, and interaction with the AVC ceiling parts all need to be resolved.
