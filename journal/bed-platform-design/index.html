Two ways to support the east-west bed

I am comparing two aluminum frames for an east-west bed: a wall-assisted concept inspired by FarOutRide and a floor-supported alternative informed by Ford's guidance.

Both options use two 97 in north-south side rails, four provisional 69 in east-west crossmembers, a nominal 60 x 78 in deck envelope, and 36 in of central garage clearance beneath the crossmembers. The deck is intended to extend into the GoCode Innie pockets without loading or touching the ABS capsules.

The wall-assisted option is my current preference to investigate, not a construction decision. Its short supports do not reach the floor, so the wall attachments would carry the bed load. The floor-supported alternative remains the review baseline because Ford's racking guidance calls for a self-supporting, reinforced floor load path and limits upper wall fixing locations. Exact body attachments, HEST dimensions, headroom, Innie clearance, and obstructions still require physical measurement and qualified review before ordering.

This is a record of the build, not an installation guide. Reported completion does not establish inspection, structural adequacy, or safety.

Van context and connection inspection

Why not approve A yet? The saved A has two short vertical supports per side, not a validated minimum. Adding a third or fourth per side does not by itself establish capacity. Wall fixing design, load distribution, body metal and reinforcement need qualified review.

FarOutRide reference (new tab) uses four main vertical supports TOTAL (two per side), plus four direct north-south rail-to-body brackets (two ends per side) and separate short rear-deck supports. That is not proof that our two-per-side design is sufficient.

The reference's "BEMM compliance" section acknowledges that its design may not follow Ford's self-supporting / floor-supported recommendation. It is a firsthand example, not Ford approval or validation for our bed. Its listed rivetnut count belongs to that build, not ours.

Our W01-W04 mark full short-support interface zones; W05-W08 mark rail-end reference zones. FarOutRide uses external corner brackets with extrusion-slot T-nuts/profile bolts and separate body bolts into rivetnuts. The saved support-to-rail 4302 brackets do not model those body fixings.

Ford 2022 FNA BEMM section 5.3.1 describes upper fixing locations as nonstructural with a 30 kg maximum and prohibits relying on lining for support. This is not a bed capacity or a per-fastener allowance. Our strips end above the floor; wall gravity load is unresolved, not merely anti-rattle restraint.

Hardware compatibility and floor anchors remain unresolved. No physical verification is implied. Ford's upper wall fixing points must not be assumed to carry the primary bed load.

Schematic cargo skeleton, not Ford body CAD. Length and maximum height use Ford's nominal 2022 unconverted-van specifications. The 70.2 in width is only a legacy Ford 2015 reference, not verified for this 2022 van. Rib spacing, roof shape and the bed's alignment to the rear are illustrative. Finished floor, lining, wheel housings, GoCode pockets and actual fixing points are not modeled. Do not infer fit or drill locations.

The saved frame is about 72 in wide, wider than the provisional 70.2 in reference. This mismatch needs measured wall/pocket geometry; the reference skeleton does not establish a collision or a fit.

Each crossmember end joins a north-south rail through a saved candidate internal anchor assembly. The vertical supports use separate external corner brackets. These are distinct joints, not one connector serving both directions. The middle crossmembers use the same internal anchor concept as the outer ones, not floating external cams. A bracket-only alternative would be a different connection design, not an automatic substitution.

J labels remain visible to make concealed connections inspectable. They are not physical parts. Removing a member removes its related joint groups. Slot alignment, machining and fastening still require review.
